# Contributing to the History of AI timeline

This timeline is open to contributions from librarians, faculty, students and other libraries. Reed Library reviews every contribution and decides whether to publish it.

## Roles

| Role | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Editor | Christina Hilburger, Reed Library | Fact-checks every claim, formats and edits cards, and decides what is published on the Reed timeline. |
| Contributor | Anyone who claims a card | Writes the card they claimed. Only the person who claimed a card writes it. |
| Field reviewer | Subject experts who volunteer | Review cards in their field for accuracy and context. |
| Faculty reviewer | An instructor running a class project | Reviews their students' cards. Instructors serve in this role for their own class automatically. |

## Steps from claim to publication

1. **Claim a card.** Add a unique claim tag to the item in the project's Zotero group [needs fact: link to the Zotero group, which does not exist yet], for example `claim:jsmith`. One claim per card; check that no one else has claimed it first.
2. **Write it.** Fill in the fields described in the tagging guide [needs fact: link to the tagging guide, which is not written yet] (title, date, what happened, why it mattered, sources, and at least one item from your library's collection). Set `status:draft`.
3. **Faculty review (class projects).** The instructor checks the student's card and sets `status:faculty-reviewed`.
4. **Field review (when a volunteer reviewer is available).** A field reviewer checks accuracy and sets `reviewer:name`.
5. **Editor review.** The editor fact-checks every claim against its sources, edits for clarity and the house style, and either publishes the card (`status:published`) or returns it with notes.

Only cards marked `status:published` appear on the Reed timeline. Class projects can group their own cards with a class tag (for example `class:ENGL100-F26`), whether or not the cards are published.

A card that is not published is still a research exercise: the writer researches one topic and finds sources in their library's collection.

## Standards every card meets

- Every factual claim has a source, and every card cites at least one item from the library's collection.
- Plain, neutral wording. Works, tools and laws do not "decide" or "want"; people do.
- Debated topics show more than one view, each with a source.
- Quotes are short, exact and linked to where they were read. No song lyrics.
- Images are public domain or openly licensed, with credit.

## Credit

Published cards show "Contributed by" and "Reviewed by" in their Sources and links panel.

Students choose how they are credited: full name, initials, or "a student in [course]". A student's name is shown only with the student's written consent. [needs fact: confirm the consent form and process with the Registrar.]
